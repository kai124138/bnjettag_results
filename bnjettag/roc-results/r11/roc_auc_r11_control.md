# Round-11 control — norm-free 8-bit at the flagship scale (era-2, n = 260,000, macro-OvR)

**Why this arm exists.** Every 8-bit arm in the project carried SubLN while the binary
flagship is norm-free — and norm-free is the *better* binary configuration (W1A8-stdnn 0.8902
vs W1A8-std 0.8763). So every published W1-vs-W8 comparison, on accuracy *and* on resources,
was confounded by normalization. `r11-d32-w8a8-stdnn` removes the confound: 8-bit weights,
norms removed, everything else byte-identical to the round-8 recipe (D32/H4/L2/FFN64,
**19,201 params as trained** — architecturally identical to every other arm at this scale;
SubLN is parameter-free, so removing it changes no count — standardized inputs, peak LR 2e-5, 101 epochs, ES patience 15).

Config `code/hgq2/configs/r11-d32-w8a8-stdnn.json` (generated by `configs/gen_r11_ladder.py`);
W&B `bnjettag-final` runs `r11-d32-w8a8-stdnn-s{1,2,3}`; evaluated locally with
`roc_final.py eval --input-std` on `data/val` — the same split as round 8. Arrays:
`R11-W8A8-stdnn-s{1,2,3}.npz` here. Rejection recomputed with `code/hgq2/rejection.py`.

## Three readings, all of which count against the binary thesis

**1. Removing the norms helps both precisions, about equally.** W8A8: 0.9151 → 0.9231
(+0.80 AUC pts, rejection 46.8 → 60.2, +28.8%). W1A8: 0.8763 → 0.8902 (+1.39 pts, rejection
24.6 → 31.0, +26.1%). On AUC the gain is *larger* for binary; on rejection the ratios
(1.288 ± 0.031 vs 1.261 ± 0.115) are indistinguishable. Norm-free is simply the better
configuration at this scale for both precisions — SubLN is not something binary uniquely escapes,
which is the point that matters for the resource comparison.

**2. Norm-free 8-bit is the best arm at this scale, and it beats the *normed* full-precision
baseline.** 0.9231 vs FP32-std's 0.9161 (+0.70 pts), and **126% of FP32-std's rejection at
ε_S=0.5** (143.8% at ε=0.3). **This comparison crosses the norm axis and is therefore weaker
than it looks: there is no FP32-stdnn arm anywhere in the repo.** Nothing here separates "the
8-bit grid regularizes" from "removing SubLN helps, and would help FP32 too" — the latter being
exactly what reading 1 shows for the other two precisions. The defensible claim is the narrow
one: *the normed FP32 baseline the project has been quoting is not the ceiling it was treated
as.* An FP32-stdnn arm (3 runs) is the experiment that would settle it, and has been launched.

**3. The like-for-like binary gap is much wider than the record states.** Against a matched
norm-free 8-bit arm rather than a normed full-precision one:

| comparison | ΔAUC | rejection ratio @ε0.5 |
|---|---|---|
| W1A8-stdnn vs FP32-std (the published "fair gap") | −2.59 pts | 0.649 |
| **W1A8-stdnn vs W8A8-stdnn (matched)** | **−3.29 pts** | **0.515** |

Binary retains **51.5%** of the matched 8-bit arm's background rejection — a factor of **1.94×**,
not the **1.54×** obtained against the normed arm. Per class it is worse where it matters:
**Z 22.1 vs 82.0 (27%)**, W 18.2 vs 40.9 (44%).

## Consequence for the hardware argument

The resource comparison at layer scale (the folding experiment repeated on an 8-bit layer; those
reports are not part of this repository) already showed
the fold is precision-agnostic (9.50× vs 9.31×) and binary's compiled edge is 2.66× at layer
scale / 1.18× modelled whole-model. This control removes the last escape from that argument —
neither arm needs SubLN, so the norm term cancels on both sides — and simultaneously widens the
accuracy side. **Modelled whole-model, post-fold: 8-bit costs ≈18% more LUT for ≈94% more
background rejection at the trigger's operating point.**

## Caveats

- The ladder scales (d48–d128) are still training; this is the flagship scale only.
- Best epochs 91–100 of 101 for the 8-bit arms — at or near the budget edge, so these are if
  anything *under*-trained, which would widen the gap further, not narrow it.
- The binary arm's seed spread is ~7× the 8-bit arm's (±0.0067 vs ±0.0009), consistent with
  binary sitting at a capacity/optimization limit rather than a precision one.
- This says nothing about DSP-free-ness, which remains true and remains binary's alone — it
  says that at this scale DSP-free-ness is not worth what it costs in rejection.
